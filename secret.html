Black holes are formed when massive stars collapse under their own gravity.
They can be detected by observing the effects of their gravity on nearby objects and light.
Black holes can also emit powerful jets of particles and radiation, which can be observed from
special space telescopes. Black holes can be classified into different types based on their mass
and size, like supermassive black holes found at the centers of galaxies. The black hole is a
region in space where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape. Scientists
study these mysterious objects to better understand the universe. When you approach a black hole,
you would experience extreme gravitational forces, a process known as spaghettification.
